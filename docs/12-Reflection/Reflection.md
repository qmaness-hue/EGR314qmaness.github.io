## Review of Module's Success
Looking back, the module was very nearly successful, but a few oversites prevented the module from reaching its full potential. A significant requirement that was missed was a long-standing actuator, as the motors only worked for about a minute before the motor drivers failed--this failure was caused by multiple separate mistakes. We also never made a physical mock-up scope or anything to indicate at a glance what the function of the system is. Other than the motors and the model of the system, I was able to accomplish everything I set out to, such as a regulated voltage, a programmed/programmable microcontroller, etc..

## Microcontroller/Module Startup Tips
1. Write down everything you plan on doing on your schematic--especially notable things (such as the motors taking 12 volts).
2. Ask for lots and lots of help
3. Check every footprint and make sure they make sense for the component

## Lessons Learned
1. Note the important but easily overlooked specifications you come across.
2. Do not rush through anything, even the smallest diode and its footprint.
3. Use testpoints, jumpers, etc. to make your system easily adjustable.
4. If you don't have something you need, immediately ask around or purchase it.
5. Time moves faster than you think.
6. Make sure the whole system works, and not just the parts you are most interested in.
7. When using components robotics students know more about, ask them to look over your work.
8. Make sure 100% that the solder-pasted components are attached perfectly, as you cannot redo them later.
9. Find a way to eat food and simultaneously get your work done.
10. Tell people about your struggles--even if you don't think they can help, they can at the very least help you think it through.
